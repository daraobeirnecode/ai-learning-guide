# GIS + AI 50-Offer Delivery Handbook — Start Here

**Purpose:** translate the commercial ranking into practical infrastructure, technology, delivery, verification, and solo-business boundaries.  
**Date:** 2026-08-23  
**Status:** implementation runbooks; build-not-executed until an individual offer is exercised on a real paid pilot.

## Bottom line

A one-person business **cannot responsibly sell all 50 as full production capabilities at once**. A solo consultant can own many narrow audits and pilots, but consequential production work requires client staff, licensed systems, domain reviewers, field crews, or specialist partners.

Current feasibility classification:

- **22 Solo-ready** — one consultant can own a bounded delivery.
- **20 Solo-pilot-only** — one consultant can build the pilot; production requires client or specialist participation.
- **7 Partner-led** — include the qualified partner in the proposal from day one.
- **1 Not viable solo** — the fully autonomous GIS-agent platform must be reduced to one bounded workflow.

## What the operator should actually sell

Do not publish a 50-item menu. Use three front doors:

1. **GIS Workflow Reliability Sprint** — combines spatial ETL, GIS data QA, regression testing, and a controlled handoff. Best pure solo offer.
2. **Field Operations Reliability Sprint** — one form, one asset/inspection type, one exception queue, and one approved report. Solo pilot with client field users and a domain owner.
3. **Cited Site Evidence Dossier** — one site decision, authoritative source register, deterministic constraints map, uncertainty, and qualified review. Solo technical delivery with a professional reviewer where required.

Use the ArcGIS governance audit as an easy read-only diagnostic and conversion path into one of those three implementations.

## Feasibility meanings

- **Solo-ready:** the operator can be the only external delivery consultant for a bounded engagement. The client still provides access, owners, users, and approvals.
- **Solo-pilot-only:** the operator can build a safe proof or pilot; production integration or consequential use needs additional client/specialist roles.
- **Partner-led:** the outside specialist is part of the delivery team and scope from the start.
- **Not viable solo:** do not sell the broad product. Narrow it to one task with explicit authority and acceptance tests.

## Capacity rule

A solo business should carry one substantial implementation at a time, or one small audit alongside one controlled pilot. Do not promise 24/7 support, incident command, field staffing, production ML operations, or regulated professional decisions without named coverage.

- accessibility specialist for independent review where legal certification is requested;
- Windows ArcGIS Pro/ArcPy worker or Esri specialist when the client cannot provide one.

## How to select an offer

Before opening a runbook, require all five gates:

1. A named buyer with a real deadline or recurring workflow.
2. A reachable data owner and representative sample.
3. A bounded paid artifact—not “AI transformation.”
4. A human reviewer with authority to accept or reject the output.
5. A measurable baseline and kill condition.

If any gate is missing, conduct discovery only. Do not build infrastructure speculatively.

## Handbook status language

- **Runbook written:** implementation sequence exists.
- **Synthetic proof exercised:** a representative fixture passed.
- **Client pilot completed:** real client UAT and acceptance evidence exist.
- **Repeatable offer:** multiple paid clients used substantially the same workflow.
- **Product:** onboarding, permissions, support, upgrades, reliability, and distribution work without hidden bespoke services.

At creation, these runbooks are **written specifications**, not completed client implementations.
